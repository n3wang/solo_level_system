### Case 1 — Coffee Chain Performance

A coffee chain operates 127 stores.

| Metric                        |        2024 |        2025 |
| ----------------------------- | ----------: | ----------: |
| Number of stores              |          98 |         127 |
| Annual revenue                | $23,847,291 | $34,192,638 |
| Operating costs               | $19,384,572 | $27,691,483 |
| Customers                     |   3,928,471 |   5,614,829 |
| Average price per transaction |       $6.07 |       $6.09 |

**Questions**

1. What was the company's operating profit in 2024?
   - **Formula:** Operating profit = Revenue − Operating costs
   - **Solution:** $23,847,291 − $19,384,572 = **$4,462,719**

2. What was its operating profit in 2025?
   - **Formula:** Operating profit = Revenue − Operating costs
   - **Solution:** $34,192,638 − $27,691,483 = **$6,501,155**

3. What was the company's profit margin in each year?
   - **Formula:** Profit margin = Operating profit ÷ Revenue
   - **Solution:** 2024: $4,462,719 ÷ $23,847,291 = **18.71%**; 2025: $6,501,155 ÷ $34,192,638 = **19.01%**

4. By what percentage did revenue increase from 2024 to 2025?
   - **Formula:** % change = (New − Old) ÷ Old
   - **Solution:** ($34,192,638 − $23,847,291) ÷ $23,847,291 = **43.38%**

5. By what percentage did operating profit increase?
   - **Formula:** % change = (New − Old) ÷ Old
   - **Solution:** ($6,501,155 − $4,462,719) ÷ $4,462,719 = **45.68%**

6. Revenue increased by more than the number of stores. What was the percentage increase in revenue **per store**?
   - **Formula:** Revenue per store = Revenue ÷ Stores; then % change = (New − Old) ÷ Old
   - **Solution:** 2024: $23,847,291 ÷ 98 = $243,339.70; 2025: $34,192,638 ÷ 127 = $269,233.37; ($269,233.37 − $243,339.70) ÷ $243,339.70 = **10.64%**

7. What was the average revenue per customer in 2025?
   - **Formula:** Revenue per customer = Revenue ÷ Customers
   - **Solution:** $34,192,638 ÷ 5,614,829 = **$6.09**

8. If the company wants to increase 2025 profit by 25% while keeping revenue constant, what would its maximum operating costs be?
   - **Formula:** Target profit = Current profit × 1.25; Max costs = Revenue − Target profit
   - **Solution:** Target profit = $6,501,155 × 1.25 = $8,126,443.75; Max costs = $34,192,638 − $8,126,443.75 = **$26,066,194.25**

# Case 2 — Manufacturing Plant

A manufacturer produces three products at one factory.

| Product | Units Produced | Price / Unit | Variable Cost / Unit |
| ------- | -------------: | -----------: | -------------------: |
| A       |         41,283 |       $53.47 |               $31.86 |
| B       |         24,719 |       $82.93 |               $49.17 |
| C       |         14,856 |      $117.64 |               $73.28 |

The factory has fixed costs of **$1,247,839 per year**.

**Questions**

9. What is the revenue generated by Product A?
   - **Formula:** Revenue = Units × Price
   - **Solution:** 41,283 × $53.47 = **$2,207,402.01**

10. What is the contribution margin per unit for Product C?
    - **Formula:** CM / unit = Price − Variable cost
    - **Solution:** $117.64 − $73.28 = **$44.36**

11. What is the total contribution margin generated by Product B?
    - **Formula:** Total CM = Units × (Price − Variable cost)
    - **Solution:** 24,719 × ($82.93 − $49.17) = 24,719 × $33.76 = **$834,513.44**

12. What is the company's total revenue?
    - **Formula:** Total revenue = Σ (Units × Price)
    - **Solution:** A: $2,207,402.01; B: 24,719 × $82.93 = $2,049,456.67; C: 14,856 × $117.64 = $1,748,149.84; Total = **$6,005,008.52**

13. What is its total variable cost?
    - **Formula:** Total VC = Σ (Units × Variable cost)
    - **Solution:** A: 41,283 × $31.86 = $1,315,276.38; B: 24,719 × $49.17 = $1,215,433.23; C: 14,856 × $73.28 = $1,088,647.68; Total = **$3,619,357.29**

14. What is its total profit after fixed costs?
    - **Formula:** Profit = Total revenue − Total VC − Fixed costs
    - **Solution:** $6,005,008.52 − $3,619,357.29 − $1,247,839 = **$1,137,812.23**

15. What percentage of total revenue comes from Product C?
    - **Formula:** Share = Product C revenue ÷ Total revenue
    - **Solution:** $1,748,149.84 ÷ $6,005,008.52 = **29.10%**

16. What percentage of total contribution margin comes from Product C?
    - **Formula:** Share = Product C CM ÷ Total CM; Total CM = Revenue − VC
    - **Solution:** C CM = 14,856 × $44.36 = $659,012.16; Total CM = $6,005,008.52 − $3,619,357.29 = $2,385,651.23; $659,012.16 ÷ $2,385,651.23 = **27.62%**

17. If Product A's price increases by 10% but its volume falls by 5%, what will its new revenue be?
    - **Formula:** New revenue = Units × 0.95 × Price × 1.10
    - **Solution:** 41,283 × 0.95 × $53.47 × 1.10 = **$2,306,735.10**

18. If Product B's variable cost increases by 20%, how much will total company profit decrease, assuming volumes stay constant?
    - **Formula:** Profit decrease = Units × (Variable cost × 0.20)
    - **Solution:** 24,719 × ($49.17 × 0.20) = 24,719 × $9.834 = **$243,086.65**

# Case 4 — Factory Capacity

A factory operates 3 production lines.

| Line | Maximum Capacity / Day | Current Production / Day |
| ---- | ---------------------: | -----------------------: |
| A    |            9,847 units |                    7,928 |
| B    |           14,639 units |                   11,483 |
| C    |           19,274 units |                   13,857 |

The factory operates **247 days per year**.

**Questions**

27. What is the utilization rate of Line A?
    - **Formula:** Utilization = Current ÷ Capacity
    - **Solution:** 7,928 ÷ 9,847 = **80.51%**

28. What is the utilization rate of Line C?
    - **Formula:** Utilization = Current ÷ Capacity
    - **Solution:** 13,857 ÷ 19,274 = **71.89%**

29. What is the overall factory utilization rate?
    - **Formula:** Utilization = Total current ÷ Total capacity
    - **Solution:** (7,928 + 11,483 + 13,857) ÷ (9,847 + 14,639 + 19,274) = 33,268 ÷ 43,760 = **76.02%**

30. How many units does the factory currently produce per year?
    - **Formula:** Annual production = Daily production × Days
    - **Solution:** 33,268 × 247 = **8,217,196**

31. What is the factory's maximum annual production?
    - **Formula:** Annual max = Daily capacity × Days
    - **Solution:** 43,760 × 247 = **10,808,720**

32. How many additional units could the factory produce annually without adding capacity?
    - **Formula:** Spare = (Capacity − Current) × Days
    - **Solution:** (43,760 − 33,268) × 247 = 10,492 × 247 = **2,591,524**

33. Management wants to increase total production by 20%. Can the factory achieve this without adding capacity?
    - **Formula:** Required daily = Current × 1.20; compare to Capacity
    - **Solution:** 33,268 × 1.20 = 39,921.6 ≤ 43,760 → **Yes**

34. If Line C increases its utilization from its current rate to 85%, how many additional units per day will it produce?
    - **Formula:** Additional = (0.85 × Capacity) − Current
    - **Solution:** (0.85 × 19,274) − 13,857 = 16,382.90 − 13,857 = **2,525.90 units/day**

35. What percentage of the factory's current output comes from Line B?
    - **Formula:** Share = Line B current ÷ Total current
    - **Solution:** 11,483 ÷ 33,268 = **34.52%**

# Case 5 — Pricing Decision

A software company sells three subscription plans.

| Plan         | Customers | Monthly Price | Variable Cost / Customer |
| ------------ | --------: | ------------: | -----------------------: |
| Basic        |     9,847 |        $19.73 |                    $4.86 |
| Professional |     6,283 |        $48.91 |                   $14.37 |
| Enterprise   |     1,147 |       $197.64 |                   $68.29 |

Monthly fixed costs are **$347,829**.

**Questions**

36. What is the monthly revenue from each plan?
    - **Formula:** Revenue = Customers × Price
    - **Solution:** Basic: 9,847 × $19.73 = **$194,281.31**; Professional: 6,283 × $48.91 = **$307,301.53**; Enterprise: 1,147 × $197.64 = **$226,693.08**

37. What is the contribution margin per customer for each plan?
    - **Formula:** CM / customer = Price − Variable cost
    - **Solution:** Basic: $19.73 − $4.86 = **$14.87**; Professional: $48.91 − $14.37 = **$34.54**; Enterprise: $197.64 − $68.29 = **$129.35**

38. What is the total monthly contribution margin?
    - **Formula:** Total CM = Σ (Customers × CM per customer)
    - **Solution:** (9,847 × $14.87) + (6,283 × $34.54) + (1,147 × $129.35) = $146,424.89 + $217,014.82 + $148,364.45 = **$511,804.16**

39. What is the company's monthly profit?
    - **Formula:** Profit = Total CM − Fixed costs
    - **Solution:** $511,804.16 − $347,829 = **$163,975.16**

40. Which customer segment generates the most revenue?
    - **Formula:** Compare plan revenues from Q36
    - **Solution:** Professional ($307,301.53) > Enterprise ($226,693.08) > Basic ($194,281.31) → **Professional**

41. Which segment generates the most contribution margin?
    - **Formula:** Compare plan CMs from Q38
    - **Solution:** Professional $217,014.82 > Enterprise $148,364.45 > Basic $146,424.89 → **Professional**

42. What percentage of total revenue comes from Enterprise customers?
    - **Formula:** Share = Enterprise revenue ÷ Total revenue
    - **Solution:** $226,693.08 ÷ ($194,281.31 + $307,301.53 + $226,693.08) = $226,693.08 ÷ $728,275.92 = **31.13%**

43. If Basic customers increase by 20%, with everything else unchanged, what is the new monthly profit?
    - **Formula:** New Basic CM = Customers × 1.20 × CM; Profit = Total CM − Fixed costs
    - **Solution:** New Basic CM = 9,847 × 1.20 × $14.87 = $175,709.87; New total CM = $175,709.87 + $217,014.82 + $148,364.45 = $541,089.14; Profit = $541,089.14 − $347,829 = **$193,260.14**

44. How many additional Basic customers would the company need to generate $100,000 of additional monthly contribution margin?
    - **Formula:** Additional customers = Target CM ÷ CM per Basic customer
    - **Solution:** $100,000 ÷ $14.87 = **6,724.95 ≈ 6,725 customers**

45. The company is considering reducing the Professional price from $48.91 to $39.47. If this causes Professional customers to increase by 50%, would monthly contribution margin from Professional customers increase or decrease?
    - **Formula:** Compare old CM vs new CM = Customers × 1.50 × (New price − VC)
    - **Solution:** Old CM = $217,014.82; New CM = 6,283 × 1.50 × ($39.47 − $14.37) = 9,424.5 × $25.10 = $236,554.95; **Increases** by $19,540.13

# Case 6 — Investment Decision

A company is evaluating two investments.

|                        | Investment A | Investment B |
| ---------------------- | -----------: | -----------: |
| Initial investment     |   $2,183,947 |   $4,872,391 |
| Annual revenue         |   $1,472,836 |   $2,947,183 |
| Annual operating costs |     $783,492 |   $1,184,739 |
| Useful life            |      5 years |      8 years |
| Probability of success |        78.4% |        61.7% |

Assume revenue and costs remain constant.

**Questions**

46. What is the annual profit from Investment A?
    - **Formula:** Profit = Revenue − Operating costs
    - **Solution:** $1,472,836 − $783,492 = **$689,344**

47. What is the annual profit from Investment B?
    - **Formula:** Profit = Revenue − Operating costs
    - **Solution:** $2,947,183 − $1,184,739 = **$1,762,444**

48. What is the ROI of Investment A after one year?
    - **Formula:** ROI = Annual profit ÷ Initial investment
    - **Solution:** $689,344 ÷ $2,183,947 = **31.56%**

49. What is the ROI of Investment B after one year?
    - **Formula:** ROI = Annual profit ÷ Initial investment
    - **Solution:** $1,762,444 ÷ $4,872,391 = **36.17%**

50. What is the simple payback period for each investment?
    - **Formula:** Payback = Initial investment ÷ Annual profit
    - **Solution:** A: $2,183,947 ÷ $689,344 = **3.17 years**; B: $4,872,391 ÷ $1,762,444 = **2.76 years**

51. What is the expected annual profit of Investment A after accounting for probability of success?
    - **Formula:** Expected profit = Profit × Probability
    - **Solution:** $689,344 × 0.784 = **$540,445.70**

52. What is the expected annual profit of Investment B?
    - **Formula:** Expected profit = Profit × Probability
    - **Solution:** $1,762,444 × 0.617 = **$1,087,427.95**

53. Which investment has the higher expected annual profit?
    - **Formula:** Compare Q51 and Q52
    - **Solution:** B ($1,087,427.95) > A ($540,445.70) → **Investment B**

54. If the company can only invest $4,872,391, could it invest in two projects of type A? What would the combined expected annual profit be?
    - **Formula:** Check 2 × Investment A ≤ Budget; Combined EV = 2 × Expected profit A
    - **Solution:** 2 × $2,183,947 = $4,367,894 ≤ $4,872,391 → **Yes**; Combined EV = 2 × $540,445.70 = **$1,080,891.39**

55. Investment B's probability of success rises from 61.7% to 74.3%. What is its new expected annual profit?
    - **Formula:** Expected profit = Profit × New probability
    - **Solution:** $1,762,444 × 0.743 = **$1,309,495.89**

# Case 7 — Retailer Profitability

A retailer sells products through three channels.

| Channel     |     Revenue | Gross Margin | Operating Costs |
| ----------- | ----------: | -----------: | --------------: |
| Stores      | $19,847,293 |        38.7% |      $4,928,471 |
| Website     | $14,638,729 |        53.4% |      $2,847,193 |
| Marketplace |  $9,728,461 |        23.8% |      $1,472,836 |

**Questions**

56. What is the gross profit generated by each channel?
    - **Formula:** Gross profit = Revenue × Gross margin
    - **Solution:** Stores: $19,847,293 × 0.387 = **$7,680,902.39**; Website: $14,638,729 × 0.534 = **$7,817,081.29**; Marketplace: $9,728,461 × 0.238 = **$2,315,373.72**

57. What is the total company gross profit?
    - **Formula:** Total GP = Σ Gross profit
    - **Solution:** $7,680,902.39 + $7,817,081.29 + $2,315,373.72 = **$17,813,357.40**

58. What is the company's total operating profit?
    - **Formula:** Operating profit = Total GP − Total operating costs
    - **Solution:** $17,813,357.40 − ($4,928,471 + $2,847,193 + $1,472,836) = $17,813,357.40 − $9,248,500 = **$8,564,857.40**

59. What is the overall gross margin?
    - **Formula:** Overall GM = Total GP ÷ Total revenue
    - **Solution:** $17,813,357.40 ÷ ($19,847,293 + $14,638,729 + $9,728,461) = $17,813,357.40 ÷ $44,214,483 = **40.29%**

60. What is the operating margin?
    - **Formula:** Operating margin = Operating profit ÷ Total revenue
    - **Solution:** $8,564,857.40 ÷ $44,214,483 = **19.37%**

61. What percentage of total revenue comes from the website?
    - **Formula:** Share = Website revenue ÷ Total revenue
    - **Solution:** $14,638,729 ÷ $44,214,483 = **33.11%**

62. What would the company's overall gross margin be if 10% of Store revenue shifted to the Website, with total revenue unchanged?
    - **Formula:** Shift = 0.10 × Store revenue; New GP = (Store − Shift) × 38.7% + (Website + Shift) × 53.4% + Marketplace GP; New GM = New GP ÷ Total revenue
    - **Solution:** Shift = $1,984,729.30; New GP = ($17,862,563.70 × 0.387) + ($16,623,458.30 × 0.534) + $2,315,373.72 = $6,912,812.15 + $8,876,926.73 + $2,315,373.72 = $18,105,112.60; GM = $18,105,112.60 ÷ $44,214,483 = **40.95%**

63. Which channel has the highest operating margin?
    - **Formula:** Operating margin = (Gross profit − Operating costs) ÷ Revenue
    - **Solution:** Stores: ($7,680,902.39 − $4,928,471) ÷ $19,847,293 = 13.87%; Website: ($7,817,081.29 − $2,847,193) ÷ $14,638,729 = 33.95%; Marketplace: ($2,315,373.72 − $1,472,836) ÷ $9,728,461 = 8.66% → **Website (33.95%)**

64. If Marketplace revenue increases by 40% while its gross margin and operating costs remain unchanged, what is its new operating profit?
    - **Formula:** New op. profit = (Revenue × 1.40 × Gross margin) − Operating costs
    - **Solution:** ($9,728,461 × 1.40 × 0.238) − $1,472,836 = $3,241,523.21 − $1,472,836 = **$1,768,687.21**

# Case 8 — Workforce Productivity

A consulting company has the following workforce.

| Level     | Employees | Revenue / Employee | Salary / Employee |
| --------- | --------: | -----------------: | ----------------: |
| Junior    |        63 |            $97,483 |           $58,729 |
| Mid-level |        28 |           $183,947 |           $97,284 |
| Senior    |        11 |           $294,183 |          $178,492 |

Other annual operating expenses are **$1,847,293**.

**Questions**

65. What is total annual revenue?
    - **Formula:** Revenue = Σ (Employees × Revenue / employee)
    - **Solution:** (63 × $97,483) + (28 × $183,947) + (11 × $294,183) = $6,141,429 + $5,150,516 + $3,236,013 = **$14,527,958**

66. What is total salary expense?
    - **Formula:** Salary = Σ (Employees × Salary / employee)
    - **Solution:** (63 × $58,729) + (28 × $97,284) + (11 × $178,492) = $3,699,927 + $2,723,952 + $1,963,412 = **$8,387,291**

67. What is total profit before taxes?
    - **Formula:** Profit = Revenue − Salary − Other expenses
    - **Solution:** $14,527,958 − $8,387,291 − $1,847,293 = **$4,293,374**

68. What is the average revenue per employee?
    - **Formula:** Average = Total revenue ÷ Total employees
    - **Solution:** $14,527,958 ÷ (63 + 28 + 11) = $14,527,958 ÷ 102 = **$142,430.96**

69. What percentage of employees are Senior?
    - **Formula:** % = Senior employees ÷ Total employees
    - **Solution:** 11 ÷ 102 = **10.78%**

70. What percentage of total revenue is generated by Senior employees?
    - **Formula:** % = Senior revenue ÷ Total revenue
    - **Solution:** $3,236,013 ÷ $14,527,958 = **22.27%**

71. What is the weighted average salary per employee?
    - **Formula:** Weighted avg = Total salary ÷ Total employees
    - **Solution:** $8,387,291 ÷ 102 = **$82,228.34**

72. If 10 Junior employees are promoted to Mid-level, and their revenue and salary change accordingly, how does total profit change?
    - **Formula:** Δ Profit = 10 × [(Mid revenue − Junior revenue) − (Mid salary − Junior salary)]
    - **Solution:** 10 × [($183,947 − $97,483) − ($97,284 − $58,729)] = 10 × ($86,464 − $38,555) = 10 × $47,909 = **+$479,090**

73. Management wants to increase total revenue by 15% without hiring anyone. By what percentage would revenue per employee need to increase?
    - **Formula:** With headcount fixed, % change in revenue per employee = % change in total revenue
    - **Solution:** **15%**

# Case 9 — Product Portfolio

A consumer goods company has four products.

| Product |     Revenue | Profit Margin | Revenue Growth |
| ------- | ----------: | ------------: | -------------: |
| Alpha   | $38,472,183 |          9.7% |           4.8% |
| Beta    | $29,847,291 |         18.4% |           9.3% |
| Gamma   | $21,638,729 |         27.6% |          -4.7% |
| Delta   |  $9,728,461 |         47.3% |          18.7% |

**Questions**

74. What is the profit generated by each product?
    - **Formula:** Profit = Revenue × Profit margin
    - **Solution:** Alpha: $38,472,183 × 0.097 = **$3,731,801.75**; Beta: $29,847,291 × 0.184 = **$5,491,901.54**; Gamma: $21,638,729 × 0.276 = **$5,972,289.20**; Delta: $9,728,461 × 0.473 = **$4,601,562.05**

75. What is total company profit?
    - **Formula:** Total profit = Σ Product profits
    - **Solution:** $3,731,801.75 + $5,491,901.54 + $5,972,289.20 + $4,601,562.05 = **$19,797,554.55**

76. What is the company's weighted average profit margin?
    - **Formula:** Wtd margin = Total profit ÷ Total revenue
    - **Solution:** $19,797,554.55 ÷ ($38,472,183 + $29,847,291 + $21,638,729 + $9,728,461) = $19,797,554.55 ÷ $99,686,664 = **19.86%**

77. Which product contributes the largest amount of profit?
    - **Formula:** Compare absolute profits from Q74
    - **Solution:** Gamma ($5,972,289.20) → **Gamma**

78. Which product has the highest profit margin?
    - **Formula:** Compare stated margins
    - **Solution:** Delta 47.3% → **Delta**

79. If each product grows according to the rates shown, what will total revenue be next year?
    - **Formula:** Next revenue = Σ [Revenue × (1 + Growth)]
    - **Solution:** Alpha: $40,318,847.78; Beta: $32,623,089.06; Gamma: $20,621,708.74; Delta: $11,547,683.21; Total = **$105,111,328.79**

80. What will next year's profit be if profit margins remain unchanged?
    - **Formula:** Next profit = Σ [Next revenue × Margin]
    - **Solution:** ($40,318,847.78 × 0.097) + ($32,623,089.06 × 0.184) + ($20,621,708.74 × 0.276) + ($11,547,683.21 × 0.473) = **$21,067,222.39**

81. What percentage of next year's profit will come from Delta?
    - **Formula:** Share = Delta next profit ÷ Total next profit
    - **Solution:** ($11,547,683.21 × 0.473) ÷ $21,067,222.39 = $5,462,054.16 ÷ $21,067,222.39 = **25.93%**

82. If Gamma's revenue falls another 10% next year but its margin stays at 27.6%, how much profit will the company lose compared with the original projection?
    - **Formula:** Loss = Original Gamma next profit − (Original Gamma next revenue × 0.90 × 0.276)
    - **Solution:** Original Gamma next profit = $20,621,708.74 × 0.276 = $5,691,591.61; Alt = $20,621,708.74 × 0.90 × 0.276 = $5,122,432.45; Loss = **$569,159.16**

# Case 10 — Growth & CAGR

A company has grown substantially over five years.

| Year |     Revenue |
| ---- | ----------: |
| 2021 | $47,283,916 |
| 2022 | $58,729,471 |
| 2023 | $71,847,293 |
| 2024 | $89,384,572 |
| 2025 | $97,638,729 |

**Questions**

83. What was the percentage growth from 2021 to 2022?
    - **Formula:** % growth = (New − Old) ÷ Old
    - **Solution:** ($58,729,471 − $47,283,916) ÷ $47,283,916 = **24.21%**

84. What was the percentage growth from 2022 to 2023?
    - **Formula:** % growth = (New − Old) ÷ Old
    - **Solution:** ($71,847,293 − $58,729,471) ÷ $58,729,471 = **22.34%**

85. What was the percentage growth from 2023 to 2024?
    - **Formula:** % growth = (New − Old) ÷ Old
    - **Solution:** ($89,384,572 − $71,847,293) ÷ $71,847,293 = **24.41%**

86. What was the percentage growth from 2024 to 2025?
    - **Formula:** % growth = (New − Old) ÷ Old
    - **Solution:** ($97,638,729 − $89,384,572) ÷ $89,384,572 = **9.23%**

87. What was the total percentage increase from 2021 to 2025?
    - **Formula:** % increase = (2025 − 2021) ÷ 2021
    - **Solution:** ($97,638,729 − $47,283,916) ÷ $47,283,916 = **106.49%**

88. What was the approximate CAGR from 2021 to 2025?
    - **Formula:** CAGR = (Ending ÷ Beginning)^(1/n) − 1, where n = 4 years
    - **Solution:** ($97,638,729 ÷ $47,283,916)^(1/4) − 1 = **19.87%**

89. If revenue grows at the same CAGR for another 3 years, approximately what would revenue be in 2028?
    - **Formula:** 2028 revenue = 2025 revenue × (1 + CAGR)^3
    - **Solution:** $97,638,729 × (1.1987)^3 ≈ **$168,191,339**

90. Which period had the largest absolute increase in revenue?
    - **Formula:** Absolute increase = New − Old; compare periods
    - **Solution:** 2021→22: $11,445,555; 2022→23: $13,117,822; 2023→24: $17,537,279; 2024→25: $8,254,157 → **2023 to 2024**

# Case 11 — The "Messy Table" Case

A company operates in four countries.

| Country | Population | Market Penetration | Customers |       Revenue | Profit Margin |
| ------- | ---------: | -----------------: | --------: | ------------: | ------------: |
| US      | 48,729,183 |               7.8% | 3,800,876 | $783,492,183 |         18.7% |
| Germany | 19,847,291 |               9.4% | 1,865,645 | $294,183,947 |         14.3% |
| France  | 14,638,729 |               5.7% |   834,408 | $178,492,836 |         23.8% |
| Spain   |  9,728,461 |               4.8% |   466,966 |  $72,847,291 |         28.4% |

**Questions**

91. Verify whether the stated number of customers is consistent with population and penetration for each country.
    - **Formula:** Implied customers = Population × Penetration
    - **Solution:** US: 48,729,183 × 0.078 = 3,800,876.27 ≈ 3,800,876; Germany: 19,847,291 × 0.094 = 1,865,645.35 ≈ 1,865,645; France: 14,638,729 × 0.057 = 834,407.55 ≈ 834,408; Spain: 9,728,461 × 0.048 = 466,966.13 ≈ 466,966 → **Yes, all consistent (rounding)**

92. What is the company's total revenue?
    - **Formula:** Total revenue = Σ Country revenue
    - **Solution:** $783,492,183 + $294,183,947 + $178,492,836 + $72,847,291 = **$1,329,016,257**

93. What is its total profit?
    - **Formula:** Profit = Σ (Revenue × Margin)
    - **Solution:** ($783,492,183 × 0.187) + ($294,183,947 × 0.143) + ($178,492,836 × 0.238) + ($72,847,291 × 0.284) = $146,513,038.22 + $42,068,304.42 + $42,481,294.97 + $20,688,630.64 = **$251,751,268.25**

94. What is the weighted average profit margin?
    - **Formula:** Wtd margin = Total profit ÷ Total revenue
    - **Solution:** $251,751,268.25 ÷ $1,329,016,257 = **18.94%**

95. Which country generates the most profit?
    - **Formula:** Compare country profits from Q93
    - **Solution:** US $146,513,038.22 → **US**

96. Which country has the highest revenue per customer?
    - **Formula:** Revenue per customer = Revenue ÷ Customers
    - **Solution:** US: $206.13; Germany: $157.68; France: $213.92; Spain: $156.00 → **France ($213.92)**

97. If Spain increases penetration from 4.8% to 7.9%, how many additional customers would that create?
    - **Formula:** Additional = Population × (New pen − Old pen)
    - **Solution:** 9,728,461 × (0.079 − 0.048) = 9,728,461 × 0.031 = **301,582.29 ≈ 301,582**

98. If each new Spanish customer generates the current Spanish average revenue per customer, how much additional revenue would this generate?
    - **Formula:** Additional revenue = Additional customers × (Spain revenue ÷ Spain customers)
    - **Solution:** 301,582.29 × ($72,847,291 ÷ 466,966) = 301,582.29 × $156.00 = **$47,047,241.64**

99. If France's revenue increases by 25% while its profit margin stays constant, how much additional profit would be generated?
    - **Formula:** Additional profit = Revenue × 0.25 × Margin
    - **Solution:** $178,492,836 × 0.25 × 0.238 = **$10,620,323.74**

100. If management wants to increase total company profit by 10%, approximately how much additional profit must be generated?
     - **Formula:** Additional profit = Total profit × 0.10
     - **Solution:** $251,751,268.25 × 0.10 = **$25,175,126.83**

# Case 12 — Integrated Case

You are advising a company considering launching a new product.

### Market

| Metric                          |           Value |
| ------------------------------- | --------------: |
| Total market                    | $1,847,293,816 |
| Expected annual growth          |            7.8% |
| Largest competitor share        |           28.4% |
| Second-largest competitor share |           19.7% |

### Proposed Product

| Metric                   |      Year 1 |
| ------------------------ | ----------: |
| Expected customers       |     187,293 |
| Annual price             |     $487.64 |
| Variable cost / customer |     $291.83 |
| Fixed launch costs       | $18,472,839 |
| Marketing costs          |  $9,728,461 |

The company expects **14.7% of customers to cancel before renewing each year**.

**Questions**

101. What is the company's Year 1 revenue?
     - **Formula:** Revenue = Customers × Price
     - **Solution:** 187,293 × $487.64 = **$91,331,558.52**

102. What is the contribution margin per customer?
     - **Formula:** CM / customer = Price − Variable cost
     - **Solution:** $487.64 − $291.83 = **$195.81**

103. What is total contribution margin?
     - **Formula:** Total CM = Customers × CM per customer
     - **Solution:** 187,293 × $195.81 = **$36,673,842.33**

104. What is Year 1 profit after launch and marketing costs?
     - **Formula:** Profit = Total CM − Launch costs − Marketing costs
     - **Solution:** $36,673,842.33 − $18,472,839 − $9,728,461 = **$8,472,542.33**

105. What market share would the product have if the $1,847,293,816 market figure represents Year 1?
     - **Formula:** Share = Company revenue ÷ Total market
     - **Solution:** $91,331,558.52 ÷ $1,847,293,816 = **4.94%**

106. What is the company's relative market share compared with the largest competitor?
     - **Formula:** Relative share = Company share ÷ Largest competitor share
     - **Solution:** 4.94% ÷ 28.4% = **0.174×**

107. What percentage of Year 1 revenue is profit?
     - **Formula:** Profit % = Profit ÷ Revenue
     - **Solution:** $8,472,542.33 ÷ $91,331,558.52 = **9.28%**

108. What is the break-even number of customers?
     - **Formula:** Break-even customers = (Launch + Marketing) ÷ CM per customer
     - **Solution:** ($18,472,839 + $9,728,461) ÷ $195.81 = $28,201,300 ÷ $195.81 = **144,023.80 ≈ 144,024**

109. If the company acquires 247,839 customers instead, what is its profit?
     - **Formula:** Profit = Customers × CM − Launch − Marketing
     - **Solution:** 247,839 × $195.81 − $18,472,839 − $9,728,461 = **$20,328,054.59**

110. In Year 2, the market grows 7.8%, but 14.7% of customers cancel. Assuming no new customers are acquired, what happens to revenue?
     - **Formula:** Year 2 revenue = Year 1 customers × (1 − Churn) × Price
     - **Solution:** 187,293 × 0.853 × $487.64 = **$77,905,819.42** (revenue falls ~14.7%; market growth does not affect retained-customer revenue here)

111. What would Year 2 revenue be if the company acquires 48,729 new customers while retaining 85.3% of Year 1 customers?
     - **Formula:** Year 2 customers = Retained + New; Revenue = Customers × Price
     - **Solution:** (187,293 × 0.853) + 48,729 = 159,660.93 + 48,729 = 208,389.93; Revenue = 208,389.93 × $487.64 = **$101,668,028.98**

112. If the company wants Year 2 revenue to be 20% higher than Year 1, how many total customers would it need?
     - **Formula:** Required customers = (Year 1 revenue × 1.20) ÷ Price
     - **Solution:** ($91,331,558.52 × 1.20) ÷ $487.64 = $109,597,870.22 ÷ $487.64 = **224,751.60 ≈ 224,752**

113. If the company spends an additional $4,872,391 on marketing and this generates 28,473 additional customers, is the marketing investment profitable?
     - **Formula:** Net = (Additional customers × CM) − Additional marketing spend
     - **Solution:** (28,473 × $195.81) − $4,872,391 = $5,575,298.13 − $4,872,391 = **+$702,907.13 → Yes, profitable**

114. What is the expected value of that additional marketing investment if there is a 68.4% probability that it generates the additional customers and a 31.6% probability that it generates zero customers?
     - **Formula:** EV = p(success) × Net success + p(fail) × (−Spend)
     - **Solution:** 0.684 × $702,907.13 + 0.316 × (−$4,872,391) = $480,788.48 − $1,539,675.56 = **−$1,058,887.08**

# Multi-table design roadmap

Design only — do not implement until schemas + question lists below are agreed.

## Goals

1. **More tables, same mental bar** — answers still need 1–3 arithmetic steps (+/−/×/÷ and small %). No Σ of huge lists unless totals are pre-shown or limited to 2–3 named rows.
2. **Teach terms by using them** — prompts name the metric (`gross margin`, `utilization`, `relative market share`); formulas stay one line.
3. **Join across tables** — questions always name the join key (company / product / segment / line). Player looks up related rows the way a JOIN uses a foreign key.
4. **Distractors stay** — unused rows train “don’t grab the shiny number.”
5. **Ugly figures** — keep non-round prices/units so estimation skill matters; scoring tolerance already scales with variable use.

## Mental-math tiers

| Tier | Ops | Typical form | Session mix |
| ---- | --- | ------------ | ----------- |
| Easy | 1–2 | subtract, divide, × then − | ~40% |
| Standard | 2–3 | YoY %, share of total, simple join | ~40% |
| Stretch | 3 + small shock | allocation, mix shift, target reverse | ~20% |

Avoid: nested CAGR roots, long multi-product Σ in one mental step, NPV/discounting (save for optional later cases).

## Join patterns (reuse across cases)

Think of each case as a tiny warehouse. Questions are SQL-shaped but spoken in finance English.

| Pattern | Join key | Example prompt shape |
| ------- | -------- | -------------------- |
| **Lookup** | entity id | “Using {product} units and the shared line setup amount…” |
| **Ratio join** | entity ↔ cost pool | “Allocate rent to {product} by unit share of shared products.” |
| **Bridge metric** | same year / company | “Gross profit from P&L ÷ Stores from Ops = gross profit / store.” |
| **Share join** | segment ↔ competitor | “Alpha’s revenue = Alpha share × total market from Market Size.” |
| **Capacity join** | product ↔ line | “{product} demand ÷ Line B capacity = required utilization.” |
| **Channel join** | plan/SKU ↔ channel mix | “Website revenue × {plan} mix % = plan revenue on web.” |
| **Target reverse** | any | “What max VC keeps CM% at X given price?” |

Implementation hint later: bindings already support `tableId` + `entityRef` (`shared0`, `slot0`, `focus`). New joins only need clearer naming + maybe a `joinKey` field on rows — no new math engine.

## Vocabulary to introduce (by case)

Keep definitions short in the Formulas modal; use the term in the question text so players learn by repetition.

| Domain | Terms |
| ------ | ----- |
| P&L / retail | Revenue, COGS, Gross profit, Gross margin, OpEx, Operating profit, Operating margin, SG&A (distractor) |
| Unit economics | Price, Variable cost, Contribution margin (unit / total), CM%, Break-even units |
| Allocation | Shared fixed cost, Unit-share allocation, Allocated fixed, Product profit after allocation |
| Market | TAM / segment revenue, Market share, Relative market share, Captured revenue, Addressable mix |
| Ops / capacity | Capacity, Throughput, Utilization, Spare capacity, Bottleneck line |
| Growth | YoY %, Revenue / store, Mix shift, Volume vs price effect |
| Risk (light) | Expected value, Probability-weighted profit (Case 6+) |
